LITERATURE REVIEW: Position Encoding in Early Layers, Removal in Late Layers
==============================================================================
Date: 2026-03-21

Core idea: Using positional encodings (like RoPE) in early transformer layers
but removing them in late layers, to improve length generalization/extrapolation.

==============================================================================
MOST DIRECTLY RELEVANT PAPERS
==============================================================================

1. "Rope to Nope and Back Again: A New Hybrid Attention Strategy"
   Authors: Bowen Yang, Bharat Venkitesh, Dwarak Talupuru, Hangyu Lin,
            David Cairuz, Phil Blunsom, Acyr Locatelli (Cohere)
   Date: January 2025 (arXiv: 2501.18795)
   Venue: arXiv preprint
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18795

   THIS IS THE CLOSEST PAPER TO THE IDEA. It proposes interleaving RoPE and
   NoPE (no positional encoding) layers in a transformer. Key findings:
   - NoPE layers naturally specialize in information RETRIEVAL (content-based)
   - RoPE layers naturally specialize in LOCAL CONTEXT modeling (positional)
   - A "division of labor" emerges spontaneously during training
   - NoPE layers use global attention; RoPE layers use sliding window attention
   - Surpasses SOTA extrapolation-based RoPE models by a large margin
   - Trained up to 5T tokens; tested from 8K to 256K context
   - Retrieval score: 96.1 at 8K, 74.8 at 256K (vs sharper fall for pure RoPE)

   ARCHITECTURE DETAILS (from full paper):
   - Called "RNoPE-SWA"
   - 1:3 ratio: 1 NoPE full-attention layer followed by 3 RoPE sliding-window layers
   - RoPE layers use sliding window = 4096
   - NoPE layers use full attention (global reach)
   - 8B params, 32 layers, trained on 5T tokens
   - The full-attention NoPE layer is placed at the END of each group of 4
   - Pattern: [RoPE-SWA, RoPE-SWA, RoPE-SWA, NoPE-full] x 8

   RESULTS:
   - MMLU +2.0%, GSM8k +1.8% over full-RoPE baseline
   - 256K context: Needles score 9.97 vs baseline 8.25
   - Retrieval degradation (8K->256K): only 22% drop vs 41% drop for baseline

   KEY DIFFERENCE FROM OUR PROPOSAL:
   - They INTERLEAVE uniformly: [RoPE-SWA, RoPE-SWA, RoPE-SWA, NoPE-full] x 8
   - We propose FRONT-LOADING RoPE, BACK-LOADING NoPE: [RoPE-SWA x K, NoPE-full x (L-K)]
   - Their interleaved pattern gives every "neighborhood" of layers both local
     and global access. Our proposal concentrates all global access in final layers.
   - Possible framing: "asymmetric split works as well or better than interleaving,
     and is simpler" — OR need a different angle entirely.

2. "DroPE: Extending the Context of Pretrained LLMs by Dropping their
    Positional Embeddings"
   Authors: Yoav Gelberg, Koshi Eguchi, Takuya Akiba, Edoardo Cetin (Sakana AI)
   Date: December 2025 (arXiv: 2512.12167)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12167

   Drops ALL positional embeddings after pretraining + short recalibration.
   Key insight: positional embeddings are "scaffolding" -- needed for training
   but removable afterward. Far outperforms RoPE scaling methods for context
   extension. Validated on models up to 7B parameters.

3. "Why Are Positional Encodings Nonessential for Deep Autoregressive
    Transformers? Revisiting a Petroglyph"
   Authors: Kazuki Irie
   Date: January 2025 (arXiv: 2501.00659)
   Venue: ACL 2025 Findings
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00659

   Proves theoretically that deep autoregressive Transformers (>1 layer) can
   distinguish sequences with permuted tokens WITHOUT positional encodings,
   because causal attention creates different contexts at each position.
   This directly supports the idea that late layers don't need PE.

4. "Deconstructing Positional Information: From Attention Logits to Training
    Biases" / "Unpacking Positional Encoding in Transformers: A Spectral
    Analysis of Content-Position Coupling"
   Authors: Multiple (arXiv: 2505.13027)
   Date: May 2025
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13027

   Key findings via head-wise ablation:
   - RoPE creates a "single-head deposit pattern" in EARLY layers
   - Nearly ALL positional processing concentrates into ONE head in shallow layers
   - Ablating that single shallow-layer head causes pronounced accuracy loss
   - All remaining heads across deeper layers have negligible positional impact
   - RoPE outperforms on position-sensitive tasks but UNDERPERFORMS on
     position-agnostic tasks
   - Tested "partial RoPE": applying RoPE to some heads, NoPE to others

5. "Decoupling Positional and Symbolic Attention Behavior in Transformers"
   Authors: (arXiv: 2511.11579)
   Date: October 2025
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11579

   Proves that positional and symbolic attention behaviors are MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE.
   All heads show strong correspondence between behavior and RoPE frequency use.
   Performance can be controlled by controlling which frequencies heads access.

6. "Fractional Rotation, Full Potential? Investigating Performance and
    Convergence of Partial RoPE"
   Authors: M.A. Khan, K.P. Gummadi, M. Gupta, A. Ravichander (MPI, Microsoft)
   Date: March 2026 (arXiv: 2603.11611)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11611

   Systematic study: applying RoPE to only ~10% of dimensions achieves
   convergence comparable to full RoPE, with up to 10x memory savings.
   Consistent across model sizes, sequence lengths, and datasets.

==============================================================================
NoPE (NO POSITION ENCODING) PAPERS
==============================================================================

7. "Transformer Language Models without Positional Encodings Still Learn
    Positional Information"
   Authors: Adi Haviv, Ori Ram, Ofir Press, Peter Izsak, Omer Levy
   Date: 2022 (arXiv: 2203.16634)
   Venue: EMNLP 2022 Findings
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16634

   First major paper showing NoPE transformers are competitive. They learn
   implicit absolute positions through causal attention (counting predecessors).

8. "The Impact of Positional Encoding on Length Generalization in Transformers"
   Authors: Amirhossein Kazemnejad et al. (McGill NLP)
   Date: 2023 (arXiv: 2305.19466)
   Venue: NeurIPS 2023
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19466

   NoPE outperforms all explicit PE methods (ALiBi, RoPE, APE) for length
   generalization. NoPE can represent both absolute and relative PEs.
   SGD-trained NoPE mostly resembles T5's Relative PE patterns.

9. "Length Generalization of Causal Transformers without Position Encoding"
   Authors: Wang et al.
   Date: 2024 (arXiv: 2404.12224)
   Venue: ACL 2024 Findings
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12224

   Temperature tuning of attention heads substantially expands NoPE's context.
   Connects NoPE's generalization failure to attention distribution distraction.

10. "Position Information Emerges in Causal Transformers Without Positional
     Encodings via Similarity of Nearby Embeddings"
    Authors: Chunsheng Zuo, Pavel Guerzhoy, Michael Guerzhoy
    Date: December 2024 (arXiv: 2501.00073)
    Venue: COLING 2025
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00073

    Shows position info emerges because nearby embeddings are more similar,
    even in randomly initialized models with causal attention.

==============================================================================
LAYER-WISE ANALYSIS AND DEPTH STUDIES
==============================================================================

11. "Layer-wise Positional Bias in Short-Context Language Modeling"
    Authors: Maryam Rahimi et al.
    Date: January 2026 (arXiv: 2601.04098)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04098

    Uses attribution-based framework to analyze positional effects per layer:
    - Recency bias INCREASES with depth (later layers more position-focused)
    - Primacy bias DIMINISHES with depth
    - Early layers weight content words over function words
    - Later layers LOSE word-type differentiation
    - Profiles are architecture-specific and stable across inputs

12. "Transformer Layers as Painters"
    Authors: (arXiv: 2407.09298)
    Date: July 2024
    Venue: AAAI 2025
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09298

    Layer specialization study:
    - Lower layers: establish local/semantic primitives (SPECIALIZED)
    - Middle layers: iterate/refine global context (REDUNDANT, uniform)
    - Final layers: targeted corrections, output assembly (SPECIALIZED)
    - Lower and final layers are non-redundant; removal is catastrophic

13. "Round and Round We Go! What makes Rotary Positional Encodings useful?"
    Authors: (arXiv: 2410.06205)
    Date: October 2024
    Venue: ICLR 2025
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06205

    - First and last layers use HIGH frequencies of RoPE most
    - Middle layers prefer LOW frequencies
    - Low frequencies = semantic attention (distance-invariant)
    - High frequencies = positional attention (position-selective)
    - p-RoPE: removing lowest frequencies improves Gemma 2B performance
    - Removing low-freq RoPE creates robust semantic channels

14. "How Do LLMs Use Their Depth?"
    Authors: (OpenReview)
    URL: https://openreview.net/forum?id=Xpd6ZYm4js

    - Early layers: high-frequency token predictions (statistical guesses)
    - Middle layers: refine via contextual information
    - Final layers: targeted corrections
    - First wordpieces need more depth than suffixes

==============================================================================
HYBRID LOCAL/GLOBAL ATTENTION ARCHITECTURES
==============================================================================

15. Gemma 2 (Google, 2024)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00118

    Alternates sliding window (local, 4096 tokens) and full (global, 8192
    tokens) attention in every other layer. Pioneered the modern
    local-global alternating pattern in production LLMs.

16. Gemma 3 (Google, 2025)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19786

    5:1 pattern: 5 local layers (sliding window 1024) + 1 global layer.
    Different RoPE base frequencies: 10K for local, 1M for global.
    Cuts attention compute by ~5x, KV cache from ~60% to ~15%.

17. Longformer: The Long-Document Transformer
    Authors: Beltagy, Peters, Cohan (Allen AI)
    Date: 2020 (arXiv: 2004.05150)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150

    Local windowed attention + global attention to pre-selected tokens.
    Smaller windows for lower layers, increasing windows for higher layers.
    Dilated sliding windows in lower layers, increasing dilation in higher.

18. Long-Short Transformer: Efficient Transformers for Language and Vision
    Authors: Chen Zhu et al. (NVIDIA)
    Date: 2021 (arXiv: 2107.02192)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02192

    Each query attends to union of local window keys and global low-rank
    projected keys. Introduces DualLN to align scales between sparse and
    low-rank embeddings. 2x speed, 3x longer sequences.

19. Big Bird: Transformers for Longer Sequences
    Authors: Zaheer et al. (Google)
    Date: 2020 (arXiv: 2007.14062)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14062

    Combines sliding window + global tokens + random connections.
    Reduces quadratic to linear complexity. 8x longer sequences.

20. Mistral 7B
    URL: https://mistral.ai/news/announcing-mistral-7b

    All layers use sliding window attention (window=4096). Higher layers
    access longer effective context due to stacking. Theoretical 128K span.

==============================================================================
HYBRID SSM-ATTENTION ARCHITECTURES (ANALOGOUS DESIGN PRINCIPLE)
==============================================================================

21. Jamba: A Hybrid Transformer-Mamba Language Model
    Authors: AI21 Labs
    Date: March 2024 (arXiv: 2403.19887)
    Venue: ICLR 2025
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19887

    Interleaves Transformer and Mamba layers at 1:7 ratio.
    Analogous to local-global: Mamba for efficient sequential processing,
    attention for powerful global reasoning.

22. Samba: Simple Hybrid State Space Models
    Authors: Microsoft Research
    Date: June 2024 (arXiv: 2406.07522)
    Venue: ICLR 2025
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07522

    Layer-wise interleaving of Mamba + Sliding Window Attention + MLP.
    Mamba = recurrent structure, SWA = precise memory retrieval.
    Extrapolates from 4K to 256K with perfect recall.

==============================================================================
RoPE FREQUENCY AND CONTEXT EXTENSION
==============================================================================

23. YaRN: Efficient Context Window Extension
    Authors: Peng et al.
    Date: September 2023 (arXiv: 2309.00071)
    Venue: ICLR 2024
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071

    Frequency-aware scaling: interpolate high frequencies (local patterns),
    extrapolate low frequencies (global patterns). Different treatment for
    different RoPE dimensions, analogous to treating position info differently.

24. "Decoupling the 'What' and 'Where' With Polar Coordinate Positional
     Embeddings" (PoPE)
    Authors: IDSIA, OpenAI, U. Colorado
    Date: September 2025 (arXiv: 2509.10534)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10534

    Shows RoPE entangles content ("what") and position ("where") information.
    PoPE decouples them via polar coordinates. Outperforms RoPE on perplexity
    and downstream tasks across 124M-774M scales.

25. DAPE: Data-Adaptive Positional Encoding for Length Extrapolation
    Date: 2024 (arXiv: 2405.14722)
    Venue: NeurIPS 2024
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14722

    Dynamically adjusts PE based on semantic information and position.
    Trained on length 128, performs well at length 8192.

26. FIRE: Functional Interpolation for Relative Position Encoding
    Authors: CMU, Google
    Date: 2023 (arXiv: 2310.04418)
    Venue: ICLR 2024
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04418

    Learnable relative PE with progressive interpolation for length
    generalization. Can represent T5 RPE, ALiBi, and Kerple.

==============================================================================
SPARSE ATTENTION AND EFFICIENCY
==============================================================================

27. Native Sparse Attention (NSA)
    Authors: DeepSeek-AI, Peking University
    Date: February 2025 (arXiv: 2502.11089)
    Venue: ACL 2025
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11089

    Three parallel branches: global compression, local sliding window,
    fine-grained selective -- combined with learned gating.

28. "Optimizing NSA with Latent Attention and Local-Global Alternating"
    Date: November 2025 (arXiv: 2511.00819)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00819

    Alternating local and global attention across layers improves
    long-range dependency propagation. 50% KV-cache reduction.

29. "The Sparse Frontier: Sparse Attention Trade-offs in Transformer LLMs"
    Authors: Nawrot et al.
    Date: 2025 (arXiv: 2504.17768)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17768

    Comprehensive evaluation of sparse attention methods.
    Larger sparse > smaller dense at equivalent cost.

30. SWAA: Sliding Window Attention Adaptation
    Date: December 2025 (arXiv: 2512.10411)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10411

    Adapts full-attention models to SWA post-training.
    Strategy 3: interleaving FA/SWA layers.
    30-100% inference speedup with acceptable quality loss.

31. DeepSeek-V3 Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA)
    Date: December 2024 (arXiv: 2412.19437)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437

    Decoupled RoPE: splits Q/K into content (NoPE) and positional (RoPE)
    components. Content from compression-decompression, position from
    separate projections. Explicit what/where separation within each layer.

==============================================================================
OTHER RELEVANT WORKS
==============================================================================

32. SeqPE: Transformer with Sequential Position Encoding
    Date: June 2025 (arXiv: 2506.13277)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13277

    Learnable PE framework using sequential encoder. Surpasses RoPE/ALiBi
    baselines on length extrapolation.

33. "Attention Sinks" Research
    - StreamingLLM (ICLR 2024)
    - "When Attention Sink Emerges" (ICLR 2025, arXiv: 2410.10781)

    Attention sinks (disproportionate attention to first tokens) emerge from
    softmax normalization, not positional encoding. Relevant because sinks
    are position-dependent artifacts that interact with PE design.

34. GPT-J / GPT-NeoX / Pythia (EleutherAI, 2021-2023)

    Original partial RoPE: apply RoPE to only 25% of head dimensions,
    leaving 75% as NoPE. Early practical hybrid within each layer.

35. "Sliding Window Attention Training for Efficient Large Language Models"
    (SWAT)
    Date: February 2025 (arXiv: 2502.18845)
    URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18845

    Replaces softmax with sigmoid + balanced ALiBi/RoPE.
    Short and long sequence attention as distinct learning paradigms.

==============================================================================
SUMMARY OF KEY TAKEAWAYS
==============================================================================

The idea of "RoPE in early layers, no PE in late layers" is WELL-SUPPORTED
by the literature, and the most directly relevant prior work is:

1. "Rope to Nope and Back Again" (2025) -- EXACTLY this idea, with
   interleaved RoPE (local/sliding window) and NoPE (global) layers.
   Shows spontaneous division of labor.

2. The spectral analysis papers (2505.13027, 2410.06205) provide the
   theoretical backing: positional computation concentrates in early-layer
   heads, while late layers are more semantic/content-focused.

3. DroPE (2025) shows PE can be entirely removed post-training, suggesting
   late-layer PE is redundant even during inference.

4. Gemma 2/3, Longformer, etc. demonstrate that mixed local/global attention
   across layers is a production-validated design pattern.

5. Partial RoPE (GPT-J, 2603.11611) shows only 10-25% of dimensions need
   PE rotation, supporting the idea that most computation is position-free.

6. "Why Are PE Nonessential" (2501.00659) provides the theoretical proof
   that deep autoregressive transformers don't inherently need PE.

The gap in the literature: most papers either (a) interleave RoPE/NoPE
uniformly, or (b) study which layers use PE more. Few papers specifically
propose "RoPE in ONLY early layers, NoPE in ONLY late layers" as a
deliberate asymmetric design. The "Rope to Nope" paper comes closest but
uses alternating (interleaved) patterns rather than a clean early/late split.
