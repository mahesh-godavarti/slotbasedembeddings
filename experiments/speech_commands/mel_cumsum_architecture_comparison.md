## Key Observations

1. **Wider embedding closes the gap to CNN**: MelCumsumFixedTied at n=100 (97.0%, 37K) essentially matches MelCNNMaxPool (97.1%, 26K). The extra width lets the tied weights express more capacity per layer.

2. **Higher time resolution helps**: hop=80 with W=10 (96.5%) beats hop=160 with W=5 (96.2%) at identical param count (24,812). Doubling the mel frames (~200 vs ~101) with a proportionally wider window gives a +0.3% boost. The model converges faster too (lower loss throughout).

3. **BN+GLU dominates mag/re/im mixing**: MelCumsumFixedTied (BN+GLU, 96.2-97.0%) vastly outperforms MelCumsumMagDeepTied (mag/re/im Linear, 92.6-93.0%) at similar param counts. The MagDeep models also show much more erratic training (val_acc oscillates wildly in early epochs).

4. **Fixed vs Mod frequencies**: FixedTied beats ModTied at 40 epochs (96.5 vs 96.3% at hop=80), but **Mod fully closes the gap at 80 epochs** (both 96.8%). Mod starts ~3% behind but catches up around epoch 25 and matches Fixed from epoch 50 onward. At n=120, Fixed still leads slightly (97.3 vs 97.0%). Mod needs ~2x training time to converge but ultimately reaches comparable performance.

5. **Constant width beats progressive channels**: MelCumsumFixedTied at constant width (96.2%) outperforms MelCumsumResNet with [24,24,32,48] channels (94.9%) despite slightly fewer params. The narrow early stages appear to be a bottleneck that progressive widening can't overcome.

6. **More layers vs more width**: 10L W=4 n=80 (96.1%) matches 8L W=5 n=80 (96.2%) — no benefit from extra depth at fixed width. But increasing width from 80→100 at 8L gives a clear boost (96.2→97.0%).

7. **Untied cumsum can beat CNN**: MelCumsumFixed untied (97.3%, 161K params) exceeds MelCNNMaxPool (97.1%, 26K params), but at 6x the parameters.

8. **Convergence speed**: MelCNNMaxPool converges fastest (88.5% val at epoch 1), followed by MelCumsumFixed untied, then FixedTied, then ResNet. MagDeep is slowest, still improving at epoch 40 (hasn't converged).

9. **Window size**: W=5 at hop=160 is optimal; W=10 at hop=80 is equivalent (both cover ~50ms of context).

12. **Depth >> width of window**: W=10 L=8 (96.8%) vastly outperforms W=40 L=2 (94.5%) at identical params (~24K). Eight narrow-window layers compose richer representations than two wide-window layers. Mod suffers even more with few layers (92.6% at W=40 L=2).

13. **80 epochs improves all models**: Doubling training from 40→80 epochs with cosine 1e-3→0 gives consistent gains: Fixed n=80 96.5→96.8%, Mod n=80 96.3→96.8%. The longer schedule allows both more initial exploration and more fine-tuning.

14. **Width scaling with 80 epochs**: n=120 Fixed reaches 97.3% (tied model, 52K params) — matching the untied model (97.3%, 161K params) at 1/3 the parameters. Diminishing returns from n=80→n=100 (96.8→96.8%) but clear gain at n=120.

15. **Starting LR matters more than schedule length**: Cosine 3e-4→0 80ep (96.2%) underperforms cosine 1e-3→0 40ep (96.5%). The high initial LR drives fast early learning that a low LR cannot recover from even with 2x epochs. Fixed LR 3e-4 80ep (95.9%) is worst — no LR decay means no fine-tuning benefit.

16. **Data-dependent frequency modulation doesn't help for audio**: ResidualMod (fixed + delta) matches FixedTied exactly (96.5% vs 96.5%), whether delta_scale is learnable or fixed. The network either zeroes out the delta or uses it to negligibly perturb the fixed frequencies. Pure ModTied reaches the same accuracy as Fixed given enough training (96.8% both at 80ep), suggesting Mod converges to approximately the same fixed frequencies — it just takes longer.

17. **ModTied bias terms act as learned base frequencies**: Removing bias from the frequency projection drops ModTied from 96.3% to 95.8%. The bias in `Linear(n_embed, n_freqs)` provides a per-frequency constant that the network uses as a base frequency, with the weight matrix adding small data-dependent perturbation. Without this crutch, the network must derive all frequency information from the data, which is harder to optimize and produces worse results.

18. **Freq prediction network capacity doesn't matter**: Expanding the freq network from `Linear(80→40)` to `Linear(80→160)→ReLU→Linear(160→40)` nearly doubles total model params (44K vs 25K) but scores 96.1% — worse than standard ModTied (96.3%) and FixedTied (96.5%). A bottleneck `Linear(80→20)→ReLU→Linear(20→40)` scores 95.9%. The problem isn't network capacity — the task genuinely doesn't benefit from data-dependent filter frequencies.

19. **Bidirectional cumsum helps**: Adding a backward cumsum improves FixedTied from 96.5% to 96.8% (separate fwd/bwd params) or 96.7% (shared params) in just 40 epochs — matching unidirectional at 80 epochs. The shared variant adds zero extra parameters. Unlike data-dependent frequencies (Mod), which try to adapt *what* to filter, bidirectional adapts *when* to look (past vs future), which genuinely helps for speech commands where word identity depends on the full temporal context.

20. **More temporal resolution doesn't help**: hop=40 (400 frames, W=20) scores 95.8% vs hop=80 (200 frames, W=10) at 96.5% for FixedTied, despite matched receptive fields. Longer sequences are harder to optimize and don't add useful information — the mel spectrogram already captures sufficient detail at hop=80.

21. **Multi-phase hurts tied models**: MP2 with tied weights scores worse than single-phase (95.7-96.1% vs 96.3-96.5%) because training with max-over-phases provides sparser gradient signal. Unlike untied models which can specialize per-phase, tied weights must serve both views with identical parameters.

10. **Cumsum front-end vs mel front-end**: Replacing mel spectrogram with a frozen cumsum spectrogram (mel-scale frequencies) costs ~1.8% accuracy: CumsumSpecCumsumTied (94.4%) vs MelCumsumFixedTied (96.2%) at similar architecture. The [log_mag, re, im] → Linear embedding from cumsum is less informative than log mel, requiring a wider embedding (3×n_freqs=240 → n_embed vs 40 → n_embed) and more params (40.8K vs 24.8K).

11. **Mod slightly beats Fixed with cumsum front-end**: CumsumSpecCumsumModTied (94.8%) edges out CumsumSpecCumsumTied (94.4%) — opposite of the mel front-end trend. With the noisier cumsum front-end, data-dependent frequencies may help adapt to the input representation. Both still trail mel-based models significantly.

## ResidualMod Experiments (W=10, 8L, n=80, hop=80, 40ep)

The ResidualMod architecture adds a data-dependent delta to learned fixed frequencies:
`inst_freq = freq_params[i] + delta_scale * LayerNorm(freq_proj(h))`

The hypothesis: if Mod frequencies are useful, adding a small data-dependent perturbation to Fixed frequencies should help without requiring the network to learn frequencies from scratch.

### Results Summary

| Model | Test Acc | Params | Notes |
|-------|----------|--------|-------|
| FixedTied (baseline) | 96.5% | 24,812 | Standard fixed frequencies |
| ResidualModTied (δ=0.01, learnable) | 96.5% | 28,140 | Per-layer learnable delta_scale, init=0.01 |
| ResidualModTied (δ=0.1, fixed, no bias) | 96.4% | 28,132 | Fixed delta_scale=0.1, no bias on freq proj |
| ModTied (baseline) | 96.3% | 27,812 | Standard data-dependent frequencies |
| ModTied (no freq bias, split proj) | 95.8% | 27,732 | No bias on freq proj or LayerNorm |

### Key Findings

1. **ResidualMod adds nothing over Fixed**: Both δ=0.01 learnable (96.5%) and δ=0.1 fixed (96.4%) match FixedTied exactly. The network finds it easier to optimize fixed frequencies than to learn useful data-dependent modulation for this task.

2. **Removing freq bias hurts ModTied**: Without bias, ModTied drops from 96.3% to 95.8% (−0.5%). This suggests the bias terms in the original ModTied were carrying useful information — essentially learned base frequencies. The network was partially mimicking Fixed through the bias.

3. **ModTied with bias ≈ Fixed + constant offset**: The bias in `Linear(n_embed, n_freqs)` acts as a learned constant frequency, with the weight matrix adding data-dependent perturbation on top. Removing the bias forces purely data-dependent output, which is harder to optimize and converges slower (60.9% val at epoch 1 vs 78.9% for ResidualMod).

4. **Data-dependent frequencies don't help for audio**: Across all variants — pure Mod, ResidualMod, no-bias Mod — data-dependent frequency modulation either matches or underperforms Fixed. The speech commands task apparently benefits more from well-tuned fixed frequencies than from input-adaptive ones.

### Architecture Details

**ResidualModTied**: Shared `Linear(n_embed, n_embed)` for re/im signal, shared `Linear(n_embed, n_freqs)` for freq delta, per-layer fixed `freq_params` + per-layer `delta_scale` (buffer or parameter), per-layer BN, shared GLU.

**ModTied (no freq bias)**: Same as standard ModTied but with `bias=False` on `shared_freq_proj` and `freq_ln`. Split projection: `shared_proj(n_embed→n_embed)` for re/im, `shared_freq_proj(n_embed→n_freqs, bias=False)` for freq. Forces the frequency output to be purely data-dependent — cannot fall back to a learned constant.

## Bidirectional Cumsum Experiments (W=10, 8L, n=80, hop=80, 40ep)

Adding a backward cumsum so each layer sees both past and future context, analogous to how convolutions (in MelCNNMaxPool) naturally see both directions.

### Architecture

Each layer computes:
- **Forward**: rotate → cumsum → windowed diff (same as unidirectional)
- **Backward**: rotate → flip → cumsum → flip → windowed diff
- **Combine**: d_fwd + d_bwd → BN → GLU → residual add

Two variants tested:
- **Separate params**: separate proj and freq params per direction (31,612 params)
- **Shared params**: same proj and freq params for both directions (24,812 params — identical to unidirectional)

### Results

| Model | Test Acc | Params | vs Unidirectional |
|-------|----------|--------|-------------------|
| BidirTied (separate params) | **96.8%** | 31,612 | +0.3% |
| BidirTied (shared params) | **96.7%** | 24,812 | +0.2% |
| FixedTied (unidirectional) | 96.5% | 24,812 | baseline |

Bidirectional improves over unidirectional at both param budgets. The shared variant adds +0.2% for zero extra parameters. The separate variant adds +0.3% with ~27% more params.

For context, unidirectional FixedTied needs 80 epochs to reach 96.8% — bidirectional matches that in 40 epochs.
